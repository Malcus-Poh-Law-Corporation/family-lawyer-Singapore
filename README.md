**How to Weigh a Recommendation Sensibly**

A recommendation tells you about one person’s experience, but it rarely tells you the whole story. A friend’s uncontested divorce may have been quick and inexpensive, while yours, involving children and a family business, may be a different animal altogether. Ask the person what kind of case it was, how long it took, how well the lawyer communicated, and whether the fees matched what was explained at the outset.

It is also natural to search online for the best divorce lawyer Singapore listings show, but remember that no lawyer can fairly claim that title. Professional conduct rules require advertising to be accurate and not to make unjustified claims of superiority, so rankings and “best” lists should be read as opinions, not facts. A sounder approach is to verify the basics yourself, then judge fit in person.

**Green Flags and Red Flags to Look For**

Once you have a few names, a short first meeting can reveal a lot. Positive signs include:
•         The lawyer explains the process in plain language and checks that you understand
•         You receive a balanced view of settlement, mediation and trial, including the risks
•         Fees and likely cost drivers are explained clearly
•         The lawyer asks about your priorities, such as the children, the home or business interests
•         Team members, languages and who handles your file are made clear

**Warning signs are just as important:**

•         Promises of a guaranteed result or a specific percentage split of assets
•         Pressure to sign up immediately or to make decisions on the spot
•         Vague answers about fees or no written overview
•         Encouragement to hide, move or undervalue assets
•         Reluctance to explain who is actually responsible for your case

The last two deserve emphasis. Courts expect full and honest disclosure of assets, and a lawyer who encourages concealment is putting you at risk, not protecting you. In the same way, the court divides matrimonial assets on a just and equitable basis, so a confident promise of a particular percentage should make you cautious.

It can also help to match the lawyer’s strengths to your needs. If you have children, ask how often the lawyer handles care and control disputes. If you have a business or overseas property, ask about valuation and cross-border coordination. If language matters to you, ask who will communicate with you in your preferred language.
